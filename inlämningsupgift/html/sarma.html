Sarma

Sarma, known in Lebanon as Waraa Inab (ورق عنب), is a traditional Middle Eastern dish made from tender grape leaves wrapped around a flavorful filling. The Lebanese version is often prepared with rice, tomatoes, parsley, mint, lemon juice, and olive oil for a fresh, tangy flavor. Some variations include minced beef or lamb, while the vegetarian version is commonly served cold or at room temperature. The grape leaves are carefully rolled into small parcels and slowly cooked until tender.

History

Stuffed grape leaves have a long history throughout the Middle East, the Mediterranean, and the Balkans. The dish is closely associated with Ottoman-era culinary traditions, during which different regions developed their own versions using local ingredients and spices. In Lebanon, Waraa Inab became a beloved part of home cooking, reflecting the country's emphasis on fresh herbs, olive oil, lemon, and seasonal produce. Today, it is enjoyed across the Middle East and beyond, often prepared for family gatherings, celebrations, and festive meals. While the name sarma is widely used in Turkey and the Balkans, Waraa Inab is the familiar name in Lebanon and other Arabic-speaking countries.

Ingredients for the grape leaves and filling:

  • 1 jar grape leaves (approximately 300–400 g), drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup short- or medium-grain rice, rinsed
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped (optional)
  • ½ cup fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • ¼ cup fresh mint, finely chopped
  • ¼ cup olive oil
  • Juice of 2 lemons
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp ground allspice (optional)
  • 1–2 tomatoes, sliced, for lining the pot

For cooking:

  • 2 cups water or vegetable broth, approximately
  • Juice of 1 additional lemon
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
