Lahmacun

Lahmacun is a traditional Turkish flatbread topped with a thin layer of seasoned minced meat, usually beef or lamb, mixed with finely chopped tomatoes, onions, peppers, parsley, and aromatic spices. It is baked at a high temperature until the dough becomes thin and crispy around the edges while the topping remains flavorful and tender. Often called “Turkish pizza,” lahmacun is traditionally served with fresh parsley, lemon wedges, and sliced onions, then rolled up and enjoyed by hand.

History

Lahmacun has deep roots in the culinary traditions of Turkey and the wider Middle East, particularly the Levant and surrounding regions. Its name comes from the Arabic expression lahm bi ajin, meaning “meat with dough.” For centuries, communities across the region have prepared thin flatbreads topped with minced meat and spices, using local ingredients and traditional ovens. In Turkey, lahmacun became especially popular in southeastern cities such as Gaziantep and Şanlıurfa, where bold spices and rich food traditions shaped regional variations. Today, it is a beloved street food throughout Turkey and is widely enjoyed in Lebanon, Syria, and other parts of the Middle East.

Ingredients (the dough):

  • 500 g all-purpose flour
  • 300 ml lukewarm water
  • 7 g instant yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

For the meat topping:

  • 400 g ground beef or lamb
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 red bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp red pepper paste, optional
  • A handful of fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp chili flakes, to taste
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp olive oil

For Serving:

  • Fresh parsley
  • Lemon wedges
  • Sliced red or white onions
  • Sumac (optional)
  • Fresh tomatoes and lettuce (optional)
