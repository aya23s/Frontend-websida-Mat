Atayef

Atayef, also known as Qatayef, is a popular Middle Eastern dessert, especially enjoyed in Lebanon during the holy month of Ramadan. These small, soft pancakes are cooked on one side, filled with ingredients such as sweet cheese, crushed nuts, or cream (ashta), and folded into a half-moon shape. They can be served fresh and soft or fried until golden and crispy, then drizzled with fragrant sugar syrup.

History

Atayef has a long history in Middle Eastern cuisine, with roots dating back to the medieval Arab world. Over the centuries, it became a beloved traditional dessert across the Levant and other parts of the Middle East, with each region developing its own fillings and preparation methods. In Lebanon, atayef is particularly associated with Ramadan, when families gather to share these sweet treats after breaking their fast. Today, it remains a cherished part of Lebanese culinary heritage, enjoyed both during festive occasions and throughout the year.

Ingredients:

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • ½ cup fine semolina (optional)
  • 1½ cups lukewarm water
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • A pinch of salt

Cream filling (Ashta)

  • ½ cup crushed walnuts or pistachios
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon (optional)

sugar syrup:

  • 1 cup sugar
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp orange blossom water or rose water (optional)
