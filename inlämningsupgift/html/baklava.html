Baklava

Baklava is a rich, sweet pastry made from layers of thin, delicate filo dough filled with chopped nuts, such as pistachios, walnuts, or almonds. The layers are brushed with butter, baked until golden and crispy, and soaked in a sweet syrup flavored with lemon, rose water, or orange blossom water. Known for its flaky texture and rich sweetness, baklava is a beloved dessert throughout Lebanon, Turkey, and the wider Middle East.

History

Baklava has a long and complex history, with roots in the culinary traditions of the Middle East, the Mediterranean, and Central Asia. Its modern form is often associated with the Ottoman Empire, where layered pastries filled with nuts became highly refined and popular in royal kitchens. Over time, baklava spread throughout the region, with countries such as Lebanon, Turkey, Syria, and Greece developing their own distinctive variations. In Lebanon, baklava is traditionally served during celebrations, holidays, weddings, and family gatherings. Today, it remains one of the most recognizable and cherished desserts across the Middle East and beyond.

Ingredients (For the pastry):

  • 1 package filo pastry (approximately 400–500 g)
  • 200 g unsalted butter, melted
  • 300 g chopped pistachios, walnuts, or a mixture of both
  • 2 tbsp sugar (optional)
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon (optional)

Sugar syrup:

  • 250 g sugar
  • 180 ml water
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp orange blossom water or rose water (optional)

Decoration:

  • Crushed pistachios
  • Whole or finely chopped nuts (optional)
