Çiğ Köfte

Çiğ köfte is a traditional Turkish dish made by kneading fine bulgur with tomato paste, red pepper paste, chili, and a blend of aromatic spices. Traditionally, it was prepared with finely minced raw beef or lamb, but today, the most common version, especially in Turkey, is entirely vegetarian or vegan. The mixture is shaped into small, finger-shaped portions and served with lettuce leaves, fresh parsley, lemon wedges, and sometimes wrapped in lavash bread. It is known for its bold, spicy flavor and distinctive texture.

History

Çiğ köfte originated in southeastern Turkey, particularly in the Şanlıurfa region, and has deep roots in the culinary traditions of the broader Middle East. According to local legend, the dish dates back thousands of years, although its exact origins are uncertain. Traditionally, meat was kneaded by hand with bulgur and spices until the mixture developed its characteristic texture. Over time, vegetarian versions became increasingly popular, especially as concerns about food safety led to restrictions on serving raw meat commercially in Turkey. Today, çiğ köfte is a beloved street food in Turkey and is enjoyed in different forms throughout the region, reflecting the shared culinary heritage of Turkish and Middle Eastern cuisine.

Çiğ Köfte Ingredients:

  • 2 cups fine bulgur
  • 1 medium onion, very finely grated
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp red pepper paste
  • 2–3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1–2 tsp red pepper flakes or Aleppo pepper, to taste
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1–1½ cups warm water, added gradually
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • A handful of finely chopped fresh parsley
  • 2–3 spring onions, finely chopped (optional)

For Serving:

  • Fresh lettuce leaves
  • Lavash or flatbread
  • Lemon wedges
  • Fresh mint or parsley
  • Extra pomegranate molasses (optional)
