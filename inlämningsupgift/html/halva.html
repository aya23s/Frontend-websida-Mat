Halva

Halva is a traditional sweet enjoyed throughout the Middle East, the Mediterranean, and parts of Asia. There are several varieties, but one of the most popular Middle Eastern versions is made from tahini (sesame seed paste), sugar, and sometimes vanilla or pistachios. It has a rich, nutty flavor and a dense, crumbly texture that melts in the mouth. Other regional varieties are made with flour or semolina, butter, and sugar, creating a softer, more cake-like dessert.

History

Halva has a long history dating back many centuries, with roots in the culinary traditions of the Middle East and surrounding regions. The name comes from the Arabic word halwa, meaning “sweet.” Over time, different cultures developed their own versions using locally available ingredients, including sesame seeds, flour, semolina, nuts, and honey. In Lebanon and across the Middle East, halva is especially popular in its tahini-based form, often enjoyed with bread, alongside coffee or tea, or as a simple sweet treat. Today, halva remains a beloved part of Middle Eastern food culture and is enjoyed in many variations around the world.

For the tahini halva:

  • 250 g tahini (sesame seed paste)
  • 250 g sugar
  • 80 ml water
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract (optional)
  • ½ cup shelled pistachios or chopped almonds (optional)
  • A pinch of salt
